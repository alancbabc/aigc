# Sound

## What This Library Covers

This library is for sound effects only.

It does **not** cover:

- background music
- soundtrack selection
- score mood boards
- music genre mapping

It is for:

- foley
- ambience
- object sounds
- movement sounds
- impact sounds
- environmental motion sounds
- off-screen sound cues
- silence as emphasis

## Use For

- `director/storyboard`
- `director/test-shoots`
- future sound-design or post-audio skills

## Output Impact

- `storyboard.json -> scenes[].sound_design`
- `storyboard.json -> scenes[].shots[].sound_cues`
- `test-shoots.json -> shots[].sound_cues`
- `keyframe-prompts.json -> prompts[].sound_hint`

## AI Usage Principles

1. Sound should support story clarity, tension, and physical realism.
2. Prefer concrete source-based sound descriptions over vague mood words.
3. Design sound in layers:
   - ambience
   - character movement
   - object interaction
   - impact cue
4. If sound has no source, function, or emotional effect, it is probably unnecessary.
5. Silence is also a sound-design decision.

## Sound Layers

### 1. Ambience

The stable background sound of a space.

Examples:

- air conditioner hum
- fluorescent buzz
- distant traffic
- wind through trees
- insects at night
- rain on pavement
- room tone

### 2. Movement And Body Sound

Sounds generated by human or creature motion.

Examples:

- footsteps on wood
- footsteps on concrete
- footsteps on dirt
- cloth rustle
- breathing
- running breath
- hand contact
- body shift on chair

### 3. Object Interaction

Sounds created when the subject touches or uses objects.

Examples:

- door handle click
- drawer slide
- ceramic cup touch
- phone vibration
- knife placement
- paper fold
- key drop

### 4. Impact Cue

A focused sound used to land an emotional, physical, or dramatic beat.

Examples:

- hard door slam
- glass crack
- sudden phone ring
- metal strike
- sharp inhale
- heartbeat accent

### 5. Off-Screen Cue

Sound that pulls attention outside the frame or before a reveal.

Examples:

- footsteps approaching from corridor
- voice from another room
- distant siren
- elevator ding
- dog bark off screen
- unseen object falling

## Decision Heuristics

### Rule 1: Match Surface And Material

Movement and contact sounds should reflect the correct material:

- wood
- concrete
- tile
- dirt
- gravel
- metal
- fabric
- glass

### Rule 2: Sound Can Enter Before Image

Use sound before image when you want:

- anticipation
- dread
- attention shift
- off-screen world awareness

### Rule 3: Sound Can Continue After Cut

Let sound survive a cut when you want:

- emotional continuity
- transition support
- hidden connection between spaces

### Rule 4: Silence Must Be Earned

Use sudden silence for:

- shock
- emotional collapse
- realization
- suspended tension

Do not use silence randomly.

## Prompt-Safe Wording

Use short, source-based phrases such as:

- `soft room tone with weak fluorescent buzz`
- `rain hitting the pavement outside`
- `heavy footsteps on concrete`
- `metal door handle click`
- `distant traffic under city night ambience`
- `sudden silence after the impact`

Avoid vague phrases such as:

- `sad sound`
- `cinematic audio`
- `epic atmosphere`

## Suggested Extraction Targets

Before adding sound cues, extract:

- `ambience_base`
- `movement_sound`
- `object_sound`
- `impact_cue`
- `offscreen_cue`
- `silence_moment`

## Anti-Patterns

- treating music and sound effects as the same layer
- writing only emotional labels with no sound source
- overloading a scene with too many competing sound cues
- ignoring material and surface differences
- adding dramatic impact sounds to every beat
